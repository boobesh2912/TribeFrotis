## Purpose

LinkedIn for Tribe Fortis is not for entertainment.

It is used to:

* Build authority in **team fitness**
* Attract HRs, founders, decision-makers
* Generate inbound corporate leads

Every post should make someone think:

> “This is something we should do for our team”

---

## Core Positioning Rule

Always frame as:

* Team fitness activity (not team activity)
* Team fitness engagement (not generic engagement)

Fitness should always be present in messaging.
But don’t make it feel like a workout—keep it experience-driven.

---

## Content Format Philosophy

LinkedIn = **Visual first, text supports**

Because:

* Company page → low attention
* People don’t read long posts
* Image carries majority of message

So:

* Text = short / medium
* Image = strong, clear, informative

---

# 🧱 Master Post Structure

## 1. Hook (1–2 lines max)

Clear, sharp, scroll-stopping.

Examples:

* “Most team fitness activities fail.”
* “We conducted a team fitness session last week.”
* “Your team doesn’t need another outing.”

---

## 2. Problem / Context (2–3 lines)

Make it relatable to companies.

Example:

“Most team fitness activities feel forced.
Employees show up, but don’t really participate.”

---

## 3. Insight (1–2 lines)

Simple but strong.

“The problem is not the team.
It’s the format.”

---

## 4. Tribe Fortis Angle (2–3 lines)

Introduce your approach.

“We design team fitness experiences using games.
So people actually move, compete, and enjoy.”

---

## 5. Proof / Example (2–4 lines)

This is critical.

“In our recent session:

* High participation
* Teams actively competing
* No one sitting out”

---

## 6. Closing Line

Short takeaway.

“If people enjoy it, they engage.”

---

## 7. CTA (Soft)

* “Happy to help if you’re planning a team fitness session”
* “Open to conversations around team fitness engagement”

---

# 🎨 Visual Content System (Most Important)

Each post MUST have a strong image.

Rotate these formats:

---

## 1. Insight Graphic

Text on image:

* “Most team fitness activities fail because they feel forced”
* “Engagement increases when people enjoy movement”

👉 Clean, minimal, bold

---

## 2. Stats / Data Post

Example:

* “90% participation in our last team fitness session”
* “0 people sitting out”

👉 Numbers build credibility fast

---

## 3. Event Snapshot (Structured)

Not random photos.

Add overlay:

* “Team Fitness Session”
* “40 Employees”
* “Game-Based Format”

---

## 4. BTS (Behind the Scenes)

Show:

* Setup
* Planning
* Organizing

Overlay:

* “How we design team fitness sessions”

---

## 5. Before / After Style

* Before: passive team
* After: active participation

(Visual storytelling)

---

## 6. Poll Post

Examples:

“What works better for your team fitness?”

* Sports
* Games
* Workshops
* Not sure

👉 Good for engagement + insight

---

## 7. Glimpse Post

Short video + minimal text:

“Quick glimpse from our recent team fitness session”

---

# 📅 Weekly LinkedIn Content Mix

You don’t post randomly.

Follow this rotation:

* **Monday** → Insight
* **Tuesday** → Glimpse / Event
* **Wednesday** → Stats / Proof
* **Thursday** → BTS / Process
* **Friday** → Opinion / Insight
* **Saturday** → Event Snapshot
* **Sunday** → Poll

👉 Flexible, but this structure keeps variety.

---

# ✍️ Writing Style Rules

* Short paragraphs
* More white space
* Simple English
* No heavy jargon
* No long storytelling

Avoid:

* Over-explaining
* Big blocks of text
* Aggressive selling

---

# 🚫 What to Avoid

* “We conducted a fun event” (weak positioning)
* “Team outing” language
* Too playful tone
* No fitness angle
* No structure in message

---

# 🔥 Final Clarity

LinkedIn success for you depends on this shift:

From:

> “We did an event”

To:

> “We deliver structured team fitness experiences that drive participation”

If that message becomes consistent:

* You build authority
* You get inbound leads
* You stand out from generic event vendors
